# Gesture decoder research for the six-channel Cyton setup

Research date: 2026-10-31. Scope: six shared-reference inputs at 250 Hz; six gestures mapped to three simulation axes. Research only: no decoder, hardware, UI, calibration, or streaming changes were made. No user recordings were uploaded.

**Recommendation: benchmark a compact, regularized gesture classifier with explicit rejection and a short temporal decision rule against the current envelope-template decoder.** Start with shrinkage LDA, and compare regularized logistic regression and linear SVM on identical recordings. There is no literature-supported universally best algorithm for these electrodes, this person, and this gesture vocabulary. Filtering, gesture classification, and preventing accidental commands are separate problems.

## What is currently implemented

The task context was read before targeted source inspection. `src/emg/gesture.rs` confirms that `process()` normalizes envelopes against rest, estimates/subtracts posture patterns, fits gesture templates with non-negative least squares, smooths gesture powers, and enables the strongest gesture plus an optional compound partner. It already includes learned coupling and activation persistence. `finalize()` calculates isolation on the same medium-strength examples used for templates. `trial_result()` adjusts movement levels without appending examples or retraining templates.

Consequently, the existing isolation score is not held-out recognition accuracy, and practice can change sensitivity without improving class boundaries. An unfamiliar strong pattern can still select a known gesture. These are actionable limitations; they do not establish the cause of the user's triceps/rotation confusion.

The new placement lab records raw and processed samples locally. That makes paired offline replay possible. Preserve source, layout, channel mapping, sampling rate, preprocessing version, trial labels, timing, and validity flags in every comparison. The Cyton analog/ADC frequency response and existing high-pass/notch filters also constrain usable information: the Nyquist ceiling alone does not describe the effective bandwidth. A 200 Hz Myo study is not a matched front-end experiment for our 250 Hz Cyton; verify the configured ADS1299 response before interpreting waveform-length or spectral improvements.

## Primary evidence and implications

| Evidence | Findings relevant to this project | Limitation / decision |
|---|---|---|
| [Phinyomark et al., 2018: Feature Extraction and Selection for Myoelectric Control Based on Wearable EMG Sensors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5982518/) | Compared features at 200 and 1,000 Hz. Classification declined at the lower rate; amplitude/power features were less affected than many frequency, complexity, and autoregressive features. | At 250 Hz, begin with compact amplitude features. Missing high-frequency information cannot be reconstructed by adding a more complex classifier. This does not imply gross gestures are impossible. |
| [Smith et al., 2011: analysis-window length and real-time control](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21193383/) | In 13 participants, longer windows reduced classification error but increased delay. Their best real-time performance occurred with 150–250 ms windows. | Test roughly 160, 200, and 240 ms; do not assume the paper's optimum transfers unchanged. Measure full cue/onset-to-command latency, including filtering and dwell. |
| [Abbaspour et al., 2020: feature/classifier comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6946760/) | Compared LDA, KNN, decision trees, maximum-likelihood estimation, SVM, and MLP in 20 participants. Rankings varied substantially with feature representation. | Supports benchmarking feature/model pairs rather than selecting a classifier from a headline accuracy. Their numeric accuracy is not an expected result for our hardware. |
| [Scheme et al., 2013: confidence-based rejection](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23322756/) | Added class-specific rejection to an LDA-based controller. Live target-acquisition throughput, path efficiency, and completion rates improved over baseline in able-bodied and amputee participants. | Explicit neutral output for uncertain inputs deserves an independent ablation. It is not equivalent to the current amplitude threshold. |
| [Scheme & Englehart, 2015: confidence metrics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26737972/) | Confidence distributions differed among eight classifiers; a one-versus-one SVM ensemble beat their earlier LDA rejection approach on accuracy/rejection and false-acceptance/rejection curves. | Model confidence is not automatically calibrated correctness. Compare rejection quality, not only classification accuracy. |
| [Robertson et al., 2019: rejection usability](https://unbscholar.lib.unb.ca/items/384d2deb-f7df-401c-8e8a-571f8dcc2909) | In 24 participants using an SVM controller, rejection improved information throughput; best thresholds in that experiment were 0.60–0.75. | Those numerical thresholds are not portable to another model. Tune thresholds using separate validation trials and measure missed commands as well as false commands. |
| [Betthauser et al., 2020: temporal convolutional networks](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10497232/) | Used eight-channel, 200 Hz Myo data from 13 non-amputee and two amputee participants, including simultaneous three-DOF movements. Temporal models improved transition stability and accuracy; their subject experiment trained on the first three minutes and tested on the next three. | Strong reason to benchmark a causal temporal model later. It is not evidence of zero-shot performance, nor of transfer to six shared-reference inputs. |
| [Wang et al., 2023: targeted electrode comparison](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40846-023-00823-x) | Explicitly kept overlapping windows from a trial in the same fold to prevent leakage. | Whole-trial and then whole-session splitting should be mandatory here. Thousands of overlapping windows are not thousands of independent examples. |

## An implementable first baseline

The following settings are proposed experiments, not established optima.

1. **Causal preprocessing and quality gate.** Replay the same valid raw recordings through common-average-on and common-average-off conditions. Keep all other processing identical. Reject windows with clipping, missing packets, or invalid channels rather than impute an apparently confident gesture. Preserve the electrical reference and BIAS; software CAR is a different operation. Do not run a zero-phase offline filter that could not be used live.
2. **Windowing.** Start at 200 ms = 50 samples per channel, updated every 40 ms = 10 samples. Compare 160/200/240 ms in training/validation only. At this sampling rate, highly detailed spectral features and large autoregressive models are poor first choices.
3. **Feature ablation.** First use six log-RMS values with an epsilon tied to the training noise floor. Compare adding normalized waveform length (six additional values). Compare a conventional MAV/WL/thresholded-zero-crossing/thresholded-slope-change set only as an alternative, not a large concatenation of redundant features. Features must use band-limited samples before the final 5 Hz envelope smoothing. Thresholds and scaling must be fit on training data only. Include a separate experiment for relative channel pattern plus overall magnitude, because magnitude-only normalization can make weak resting noise look like a strong pattern.
4. **Classifier.** Train seven classes: six selected gestures plus rest. Use shrinkage LDA with shared covariance regularized toward its diagonal; select shrinkage within grouped training folds. The regularization limits instability from correlated features and few independent repetitions. Export a fixed feature transform, means, covariance-derived linear weights, class priors, and versioned preprocessing parameters. Linear scoring is small enough for browser/WASM inference.
5. **Comparators.** Keep current NNLS as the control. Run L2-regularized multinomial logistic regression and linear SVM on the same feature vectors/splits. If clear nonlinear confusion persists, compare an RBF SVM, accepting extra tuning and inference complexity. A small causal temporal CNN is a subsequent candidate once recordings include natural gesture transitions. Complexity is justified by held-out control improvement, not by model name.
6. **Reject rather than force an answer.** Require valid signal, adequate activity, and a validated class confidence/margin. Also test a per-class distance limit: a high relative softmax score alone does not establish proximity to any trained class. Treat out-of-pattern input as unknown/neutral. Thresholds come from validation trials, including negative movements; no threshold guarantees rejection of every unseen gesture.
7. **Temporal decisions.** Start with three consecutive accepted same-class decisions at 40 ms increments, and compare shorter/longer persistence. Stop or return neutral promptly on quality failure, rest, or sustained rejection; never hold the last command indefinitely through lost signal. Hysteresis can reduce flicker but adds delay. Initially use one gesture and modest fixed speed; evaluate proportional speed and simultaneous gestures separately later.

Regularized LDA is recommended as a transparent, inexpensive baseline—not because evidence proves it superior to logistic regression, SVM, or deep models. Temporal models can learn useful transition information even at 200 Hz. Training sequence models only on a fixed cue order, however, risks learning the procedure's timing instead of intended gestures; randomize order and include natural transitions.

## Data collection and benchmark

- Retain current layout as Layout A. The existing three holds per movement support screening but provide little independent data for tuning and final testing. A practical initial collection is five lab runs per gesture (15 holds), with three complete runs for training, one for validation, and one untouched run for testing. Rotate/randomize gesture order between blocks, with comfortable breaks. This is a manageable starting protocol, not a statistical-power guarantee; few test trials mean substantial uncertainty, and a second session remains necessary.
- Include all six candidate gestures, relaxed rest in several comfortable postures, triceps-only and biceps-only contractions, and other plausible non-command actions. Under the current mapping triceps may be a legitimate reach gesture; it is a negative example specifically for rotation. In a forearm-only remapping it can be labeled a nuisance/non-command movement. Do not collapse every nuisance movement into an assumed exhaustive unknown class.
- Use repeated comfortable contractions at more than one effort level. Label transition periods separately, allowing for human response delay; do not assume cue time equals muscle onset. Score both steady holds and transitions.
- Separate recording blocks into training, validation, and an untouched test block before generating windows. Keep entire repetitions together. Fit filtering choices, feature scaling, classifier hyperparameters, channel selection, and rejection thresholds without the test block. If placement is chosen after examining a block, that block becomes development data.
- Compare exactly matched preprocessing/features/models one factor at a time. In particular, removing CH3 only from classifier inputs does not remove its contribution to previously computed CAR.
- Report per-gesture recall, confusion matrix including rest/unknown, false rotation events per minute during triceps-only and rest, accepted-decision accuracy **and coverage**, onset-to-correct-command delay, release-to-stop delay, and live target completion. A model that rejects everything must not appear successful.
- Run a later session with pads removed/reapplied and the model frozen. That tests no-recalibration cross-session performance. An unlabeled baseline adjustment is still adaptation and should be documented separately. A model trained/tested on the same session is not zero-shot.

Choose the smallest model that reduces false actions without an unacceptable rejection or delay penalty on held-out trials and a live simulation task. No empirical improvement has yet been demonstrated by this research report.
